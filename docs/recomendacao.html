Seazone · Itapema (SC) · investment screen

Buy the sleeping capacity, not the address

Itapema's short-stay market pays for beds, not for postcodes. The sale market prices square metres. The whole return sits in that gap — and it is widest in compact two- and three-bedroom apartments one street back from Meia Praia, not in the studios in Centro we went in expecting to find.

The recommendation

Deploy into compact 2-bedroom apartments of 60–72 m² with two suítes, sleeping 5–6, in Morretes and Tabuleiro dos Oliveiras — the inland band 300–800 m behind Meia Praia — at asking prices of R$680k–850k. Add a smaller beach-band sleeve of compact 2BR at R$0.93–1.2M for brand presence and resale liquidity. Avoid the 3- and 4-bedroom beachfront stock, which is where 70% of the listed capital sits and where returns are worst.

The internal hypothesis

“Compact studios / 1-bedrooms in Centro are the most efficient opportunity” — rejected as stated. Not one studio is for sale in the entire dataset; 1-bedrooms are 2.0% of listed apartments and 16 units in Centro. Centro shows no revenue premium once distance to the sea and unit size are controlled, and 1-bedrooms carry the highest price per square metre in the city. The instinct behind the hypothesis is right — small is efficient — but the market has already priced it, and the executable version of that instinct is a compact 2BR one street back, not a studio downtown.

01

What the data can and cannot tell us

Two of the four business questions are answerable with real precision. One is answerable only as a range. One is not answerable at all, and pretending otherwise would be the most expensive error here.

Answerable

  • Relative revenue between property profiles and locations. The calendar file is an availability file — a row exists only when a night is bookable — so occupancy is observable, not assumed.
  • Acquisition cost by segment, from 6,796 cleaned sale ads with area, condo fee and neighbourhood.
  • Which characteristics drive revenue, with confidence intervals.

Not answerable

  • Eight months of the year. The calendar covers 6 Jan – 20 Apr 2025 only. Annual revenue requires a stated seasonality assumption; we show the sensitivity rather than hide it.
  • Transaction prices. VivaReal shows asking prices. We apply a 7% negotiation discount and sweep 0–12%.
  • Capital appreciation and tax. Excluded entirely. Every yield here is a cash yield.
  • Where a sale listing actually is. VivaReal has no coordinates — only a neighbourhood name. This is the single largest source of error in the analysis, and it is stress-tested in §06.
02

Turning a scraped calendar into revenue

Price is not revenue. Availability far in advance is not occupancy. Both corrections change the answer, so both are made explicitly.

The file was captured three times — 6, 7 and 20 January 2025 — each time looking about 90 nights forward. Comparing the same stay-date seen at two different lead times isolates how fast a night fills. We fit logit P(available) = listing + stay-date + lead-time across 209,846 listing-nights, then project every listing to a three-day lead: what the calendar will look like once bookings are essentially complete. Nights at zero-to-two days lead are excluded — that drop is the platform's own booking cut-off, not demand.

The booking curve

How much more available a night looks, in log-odds, the further out you look. A night 90 days out appears 3.1 log-odds more open than the same night three days out.

Revenue per available night, observed

Occupancy × nightly price, by week of stay. Peak Réveillon–Carnaval, then a steady slide into April. This shape is what forces the seasonality assumption for the eight unobserved months.

Stated assumptions behind every annual figure

May–September at 50% of the observed April level, October 70%, November 100%, December 110% of January. That yields an annualisation factor of 2.47× the observed 105-night window. 90% of unavailable nights are treated as paid bookings (the rest owner blocks); the host receives 90% of the displayed nightly price. Every one of these is swept in §06 — the segment ranking does not move.

Two findings are worth stating plainly. First, occupancy is nearly unexplainable (R² = 0.10) while price is well explained (R² = 0.37): in Itapema everyone fills the summer, and operators separate themselves on rate, not on nights sold. Professional listings in fact run lower occupancy and materially higher revenue. Second, proximity to the sand is worth far less than the market assumes. Revenue falls about 6% per doubling of distance; asking price falls about 9%. Moving one street inland is accretive, not dilutive — and that single fact is what the recommendation is built on.

06

Would we still buy this if we were wrong?

2,187 combinations of the seven assumptions that matter, plus the one error that could actually overturn the result.

Assumption sweep

Booked share of blocked nights (0.80–1.00), host take (0.85–1.00), low-season multiplier (0.35–0.65), furnishing (R$800–1,800/m²), management fee (15–25%), negotiation discount (0–12%), utilities (R$30–65/night).

  • Morretes 3BR ranks #1 in all 2,187 combinations.
  • Tabuleiro 2BR #2 in all; Morretes 2BR #3 in all.
  • Centro 1BR never rises above 6th; Centro 3BR is last in every run.
  • No assumption in a defensible range makes the hypothesis' segment competitive.

The error that would matter

VivaReal has no coordinates. If the off-beach sale stock is systematically further from the sea than the Airbnb listings we used as comparables, we have overstated its revenue.

  • At twice the assumed distance: Morretes 3BR still first at 7.0%.
  • At three times: Morretes 3BR 6.6%, beach-band 2BR 5.8% — the two cross.
  • At three times and a distance elasticity 2.5× our estimate: beach-band 2BR takes the lead.
  • This is why the recommendation carries a beach-band sleeve. It is insurance, priced at about 80 basis points of yield.

Honest health warnings

Only 1,005 of 4,441 Airbnb listings carry calendar data, and that subset skews toward professional, apartment, better-photographed listings — so absolute occupancy is likely flattered. Unavailable ≠ booked: owner blocks are handled with a 10% haircut, not measured. Morretes and Tabuleiro rest on 10 and 12 Airbnb comparables respectively; the Morretes 2BR cell, with 51 comparables and 827 ready listings, is the sturdiest of the high-yield segments and is why it, rather than the 3BR, anchors the recommendation.

08

Answers to the four questions

  1. Best property profile. Apartment, 2 bedrooms (both suítes) or a compact 3, 60–72 m², sleeping 6–8, one parking space, in a building with a pool and elevator, condominium fee under R$400. The design brief is maximum comfortable sleeping capacity per square metre purchased — every additional guest the unit can host is worth roughly 0.55% on nightly rate for every 1% of capacity added, while the purchase price scales with area.
  2. Best location by revenue. In absolute revenue, the Meia Praia beach band (R$1,456/m² a year for a compact 2BR against R$1,064 for Centro's 3-bedroom stock). But revenue per reais invested peaks 300–800 m inland, in Morretes and Tabuleiro dos Oliveiras, because the sale market discounts distance faster (−9% per doubling) than the rental market punishes it (−6%). Centro is neither: it is priced like beachfront and performs like inland.
  3. Characteristics that explain revenue. In order: sleeping capacity, professional operation (+27% on rate), guest rating, listing completeness (photo count), sea view, and only then proximity to the beach. Amenities beyond air-conditioning, a grill and parking add little. Note what is missing — occupancy differences across the town are mostly noise.
  4. What we would buy. R$20M across ~25 compact units, weighted 55–60% to Morretes / Tabuleiro 2BR at R$680k–850k and 40–45% to the Meia Praia beach band for liquidity. Expected unlevered net cash yield of 6.4% on segment medians and up to 9.7% on disciplined selection, before any capital appreciation. We would not buy a 4-bedroom beachfront apartment at any price in this dataset: 3.3% net, 31-year payback, and R$4.5bn of it is for sale.

One number to keep

Gross yield on the recommended segment is 11.7%; net cash yield is 6.4%. Any yield promised to an investor in this market that sits between those two numbers is a gross yield wearing a net label. The 5.3-point gap is management fee, condominium, IPTU, utilities and reserve — all of it real, most of it fixed, and disproportionately punishing on small units, which is the second reason the studio thesis fails.
